# Frequently asked questions

These are the questions people search for most about the CCAT, taken from Google's "People also ask" results for US searches on "ccat test" and "ccat score" in November 2026, plus a few that come up in every candidate's mind. Answers stick to what Criteria states, and say so when the answer is our guidance.

## What is a CCAT test?

The Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test, a pre-employment test from Criteria Corp. It has 50 multiple-choice questions in 15 minutes, covering verbal, math and logic, and spatial reasoning. Employers use it to judge problem solving and learning ability. See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## What does the CCAT do?

It gives an employer a quick measure of how well you think on your feet. Criteria says it measures critical thinking, problem solving, learning ability and the ability to digest and apply new information. The employer sees your raw score and percentile and uses them alongside the rest of your application.

## How hard is a CCAT test?

The questions themselves are mostly straightforward. The difficulty is the clock: 18 seconds per question. Criteria says the average test taker gets 24 of 50 right and fewer than 1% answer every question. Most people find it hard because they cannot finish, not because any single question is beyond them.

## How do I pass the CCAT?

There is no fixed pass mark, so "passing" means reaching the range your employer set. Our guidance on what helps most:

1. Learn every question form so you never read instructions on the day. The [section guides](../README.md#the-five-question-types-at-a-glance) cover them.
2. Practise without a calculator.
3. Learn to guess and move on. Criteria advises guessing once you have spent over a minute on one problem; we suggest 40 seconds.
4. Take at least two full timed runs before the real one.

## Is 40 a good CCAT score?

Yes, by any measure Criteria publishes. The average is 24 correct, so 40 is far above it. Criteria does not publish its full raw-score-to-percentile table on its candidate pages, so the exact percentile for 40 is not something we can quote. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## What is a good CCAT score?

Good means at or above the range your employer chose for the role, and employers rarely say what that is. Criteria offers employers custom score ranges for different positions. As our study guidance: 30 or more on well-made timed practice gives a solid margin over the average of 24.

## Is there a penalty for wrong answers?

No. Criteria states that wrong answers are not penalised. Never leave a question blank.

## Can I use a calculator on the CCAT?

No. Criteria says calculators are not allowed. Criteria's pages do not say whether scratch paper is allowed, so check your test instructions.

## Can I go back to a question?

Criteria's candidate pages do not say. Plan as if each answer is final when you move on, and check the instructions on the day.

## Can I take the CCAT on my phone?

Criteria says its tests can be taken on laptops, desktops, tablets or mobile devices, and that the instructions will tell you if a specific assessment is not compatible. A computer with a mouse is usually faster for a timed test.

## Can I retake the CCAT?

Criteria's candidate pages do not describe a general retake policy. That decision belongs to the employer. If a technical problem stopped your test and you see "You cannot restart this event again", Criteria's FAQ says to contact Criteria Support through the chat button in the assessment centre.

## Will I see my score?

Results are sent to the employer that gave you the test. Criteria's FAQ says its support team cannot access or share results, so ask the employer about your result or your application.

## Who sees my CCAT results?

Only the company that gave you the assessment. Criteria's FAQ says results are confidential and are not shared with other companies.

## What is the CCAT 7?

A different test. The CCAT 7 is the Canadian Cognitive Abilities Test, a school ability test for children from a different publisher. It has nothing to do with the Criteria hiring test. The same goes for "how do I interpret my CCAT 7 score": that is a question about a child's school report, not a job application.

## Is an aptitude test the same as a cognitive test?

The terms overlap. "Cognitive ability" or "cognitive aptitude" tests measure general reasoning: how well you learn, solve problems and handle new information. "Aptitude test" is a wider term that can also mean tests of specific skills, such as mechanical aptitude. The CCAT is both: Criteria calls it a cognitive aptitude test.

## Is the CCAT the same as the UCAT?

Criteria's UCAT (Universal Cognitive Aptitude Test) is its language-independent version: 40 items in 20 minutes, in 21 languages. Criteria reports a high correlation (.80) between the two. It is unrelated to the UK and Australian medical school admissions test that is also called the UCAT.
