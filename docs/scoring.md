# How CCAT scoring works

Short version: you get one point per correct answer, out of 50. Wrong answers and blanks both score zero, so there is no penalty for guessing. The employer sees that raw score and your percentile, which compares you with other test takers. There is no universal pass mark: each employer decides what score fits the job.

## Raw score

Your raw score is the number of questions you got right, from 0 to 50. Criteria states that wrong answers are not penalised. So a blank and a wrong answer cost you exactly the same, and a guess can only help.

Criteria publishes two numbers that put the raw score in context:

- **The average test taker gets 24 questions right.**
- **Fewer than 1% of candidates answer all 50.**

So a score in the mid-20s is normal, and a score in the 30s is well above average. The test is designed so that almost nobody runs out of questions before running out of time.

## Percentile

Alongside the raw score, the employer sees your **percentile**: where you rank against a comparison group (a "norm group") of other test takers. Criteria's example: a percentile of 45 means you scored better than 45% of the norm group.

Criteria does not publish its full table converting raw scores to percentiles on its candidate pages. That is why you will see different figures quoted online for, say, "what percentile is 35". Treat those as estimates unless they come from Criteria.

## What counts as a good score

The honest answer: good means at or above the range your employer has set for that role, and you usually will not be told what that is.

What Criteria says:

- The CCAT comes with **custom score ranges for a wide variety of positions.** An employer hiring for a role that needs a lot of problem solving can set a higher range than one hiring for a role that needs less.
- Criteria describes the score as **one of many factors** an employer considers.

You will see numbers such as "30 is a good score" or "aim for the 80th percentile" on forums and prep sites. None of these come from Criteria. What you can say from Criteria's own figures:

| Raw score | What it means, from Criteria's published figures |
|---|---|
| 24 | The average result |
| Above 24 | Above average. How far above in percentile terms is not published on Criteria's candidate pages. |
| 50 | Fewer than 1% of candidates even reach all 50 questions |

**Our study guidance, not a Criteria standard:** if you can score 30 or more on well-made timed practice tests, you have a solid margin over the average. If you are applying for an analytical role, aim higher.

## What happens to your score

- **Who sees it.** Criteria's candidate FAQ says results go only to the company that gave you the assessment. They are confidential and are not shared with other companies.
- **Can you see it.** Results are sent to the employer. Criteria's support team says it cannot access or share results, so questions about your result or your application go to the employer.
- **Retakes.** Criteria's candidate pages do not describe a general retake policy. Whether you can resit, and how long a score is kept, is up to the employer. If your test was interrupted by a technical problem, the FAQ says to contact Criteria Support through the chat button in the assessment centre.

## Why your score can vary between attempts

Criteria says the CCAT draws on an item bank of thousands of items, so two sittings will not show the same questions. Small swings between attempts are normal. That is one more reason to train the methods and the pacing rather than memorise answers.

## How scoring should change your strategy

Three rules follow directly from how the CCAT is scored:

1. **Never leave a question blank.** No penalty means a guess is free.
2. **Do not chase the hard ones.** Every question is worth one point. An easy question you reach counts as much as a hard one you solve.
3. **Speed and accuracy together set your score.** The [timing guide](timing.md) shows a plan that balances them.
