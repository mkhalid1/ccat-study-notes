# Spatial reasoning

**In one line:** you look at shapes and find the rule: which figure does not belong, which comes next, or how a shape looks after it is turned or mirrored.

Spatial reasoning is the third CCAT question type named by Criteria. Criteria's own sample shows a set of figures where one does not belong: it is the only one with two squares instead of a triangle. These items need no vocabulary and no arithmetic, only a systematic eye.

## What it measures

Spatial items check whether you can hold a shape in your head, compare several features at once (shape, number, colour, direction, position) and find the one feature that follows a rule. Criteria says the CCAT predicts how well people learn and solve problems, and spatial items do that without relying on language.

## How the questions look

On the real test these are drawn figures. Here they are shown with text symbols so they work in any reader. The forms are the same.

| Form | What you are asked |
|---|---|
| **Odd one out** | Four figures share a rule. Which one breaks it? |
| **Next in the series** | Figures change step by step. What comes next? |
| **Grid completion** | A 3 by 3 grid with one empty cell. Which figure completes it? |
| **Rotation and mirror** | How does a shape look after it is turned or reflected? |
| **Counting** | How many squares, triangles or dots in a figure? |

## A method that works

1. **List the features before you look for the rule.** For each figure ask: what shapes, how many, filled or empty, which way it points, where it sits.
2. **Change one feature at a time.** Compare figure 1 with figure 2. What changed? Then figure 2 with figure 3. The thing that changes the same way each time is the rule.
3. **In a grid, read rows, then columns.** Most grids follow a rule along each row (each shape appears once per row) or each column (the count goes up by one).
4. **For rotations, track one corner or one end.** Pick the arrow tip or the short stroke of a letter, and follow only that.
5. **Eliminate.** With five options, ruling out three is usually faster than proving one.

## Traps that cost points

- **Finding a rule that fits only some figures.** If your rule fits figures 1 and 2 but not 3, it is the wrong rule. Check it against every figure.
- **Confusing rotation with reflection.** Rotating a "p" by 180 degrees gives "d". Mirroring it left to right gives "q". Turning keeps the shape's handedness; mirroring flips it.
- **Clockwise versus anticlockwise.** Decide the direction from the first two steps, and say it to yourself.
- **Missing the big shape in counting items.** A 2 by 2 grid has five squares, not four: the four small ones and the large one around them.
- **Fixating on one feature.** If the shapes look different, the rule may still be about count or shading. Go through the feature list.

## Timing

As guidance: 15 to 30 seconds each. Spatial items are either quick or very slow, with little in between. If you cannot name the rule after 20 seconds, eliminate what you can, guess among the rest and move on.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the CCAT or from Criteria's materials. Black symbols (● ■ ▲ ◆ ★) are filled; white symbols (○ □ △ ◇ ☆) are empty.

**Item 1.** Which figure does not belong?

```
A. ■ ■ □     B. ● ● ○     C. ▲ ▲ △     D. ◆ ◇ ◇     E. ★ ★ ☆
```

**Item 2.** The arrow turns the same way each step. What comes next?

```
↑      ↗      →      ↘      ?
```

A. ↙ · B. ← · C. ↓ · D. ↑ · E. ↖

**Item 3.** Each row follows the same rule. Which figure goes in the empty cell?

```
●   ■   ▲
■   ▲   ●
▲   ●   ?
```

A. ● · B. ■ · C. ▲ · D. ◆ · E. ○

**Item 4.** A mirror stands upright to the right of the letter **b**. What does the reflection look like?

A. b · B. d · C. p · D. q · E. 6

**Item 5.** How many squares of any size are in this figure, a large square split into four equal small squares?

```
┌───┬───┐
│   │   │
├───┼───┤
│   │   │
└───┴───┘
```

A. 4 · B. 5 · C. 6 · D. 8 · E. 9

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: D.** Every other figure has two filled symbols and one empty one. D has one filled and two empty.

**Item 2: C, ↓.** The arrow turns 45 degrees clockwise each step: up, up-right, right, down-right. The next is down. *Why not A:* down-left would be a 90 degree turn, skipping a step.

**Item 3: B, ■.** Each row contains one circle, one square and one triangle. The third row has a triangle and a circle, so the missing figure is a square. The columns follow the same rule, which confirms it.

**Item 4: B, d.** A vertical mirror swaps left and right. The bowl of "b" is on the right of the stem; in the mirror it is on the left, which gives "d". *Why not D:* "q" also has the bowl on the left, but at the top with the stem going down, which needs a turn as well as a flip.

**Item 5: B, 5.** There are four small squares and one large square made by all four together. *Why not A:* it forgets the large square, the classic counting trap.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | D | C | B | B | B |

- **5:** spatial is a strength. Keep your feature list habit and move on to your weakest type.
- **4:** look at which feature you missed: count, shading, direction or position. Put that feature first on your list.
- **3 or fewer:** go through the feature list out loud on every item until it is automatic. Practise rotations with a real object, such as a sticky note with a letter on it.
