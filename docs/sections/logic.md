# Logic

**In one line:** you read two or three short statements and decide what must be true, what must be false, and what you cannot tell.

Logic shares a question type with math in Criteria's description of the CCAT ("math and logic"). These items need no arithmetic, or very little. They test whether you can stick to exactly what the statements say.

## What it measures

Criteria says the CCAT measures critical thinking and problem solving. Logic items are the most direct test of both. They reward reading precisely and punish filling gaps with what seems likely.

## How the questions look

Three forms are common in this style of test.

| Form | What you see | What you answer |
|---|---|---|
| **True, false or uncertain** | Two statements you must accept, then a third statement | Whether the third is true, false or uncertain |
| **Ordering** | Comparisons such as "taller than" or "finished before" | Who is first, last, or in a given position |
| **Small puzzles** | Seats, days of the week, overlapping groups | One fact you can work out |

For the first form, the three answers mean:

| Answer | Choose it when |
|---|---|
| **True** | The first two statements force the third to be true. |
| **False** | The first two statements force the third to be false. |
| **Uncertain** | The third could be true or false and the statements do not decide it. |

## A method that works

1. **Accept the statements as fact,** even if they sound odd. "All pilots are left-handed" is true for this item.
2. **Turn each statement into a picture.** For "all A are B", draw A as a circle inside B. For "some A are B", draw two overlapping circles. For orderings, draw a line with names on it.
3. **Try to break the conclusion.** Can you draw a picture that fits both statements but makes the conclusion false? If yes, it is not True. Can you draw one that makes it true? If both are possible, it is Uncertain.
4. **For "if" statements, go one direction only.** "If it rains, the match is cancelled" tells you rain leads to cancellation. It says nothing about why else a match might be cancelled.
5. **For puzzles, place the fixed facts first,** such as "Ben is in seat 3", then fill in the rest.

## Patterns worth knowing

| Statements | What follows | What does not follow |
|---|---|---|
| All A are B. X is A. | X is B. | |
| All A are B. X is B. | | X is A. (Uncertain) |
| Some A are B. All B are C. | Some A are C. | All A are C. |
| All A are B. Some B are C. | | Some A are C. (Uncertain) |
| No A are B. X is B. | X is not A. | |
| If P, then Q. P happened. | Q happened. | |
| If P, then Q. Q did not happen. | P did not happen. | |
| If P, then Q. Q happened. | | P happened. (Uncertain) |
| A > B and B > C | A > C | |
| A > C and B > C | | A > B. (Uncertain) |

## Traps that cost points

- **Reversing "all".** "All analysts use spreadsheets" does not mean everyone who uses spreadsheets is an analyst.
- **Reversing "if".** If rain cancels the match, a cancelled match does not prove it rained. There may be another reason.
- **Using common sense.** If the statements do not say it, you do not know it, however likely it seems. That is what Uncertain is for.
- **Treating "some" as "only some".** In logic, "some" means at least one, and it could be all.
- **Calling a contradiction Uncertain.** In ordering items, if the third statement creates a loop (Tom above Ann above Raj above Tom), it is False, not Uncertain.
- **Overlap puzzles counted twice.** When two groups overlap, the people in both are counted in each group. Subtract the overlap once.

## Timing

As guidance: 15 to 25 seconds for a true, false or uncertain item, and up to 40 seconds for a puzzle. Drawing a quick picture feels slower but is faster than reading the statements three times.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the CCAT or from Criteria's materials.

For items 1 to 3 and 5, assume the first two statements are true. Is the third statement **true**, **false** or **uncertain**?

**Item 1.** All analysts use spreadsheets. Priya is an analyst. *Priya uses spreadsheets.*

**Item 2.** All roses are flowers. Some flowers fade quickly. *Some roses fade quickly.*

**Item 3.** Tom is taller than Ann. Ann is taller than Raj. *Raj is taller than Tom.*

**Item 4.** Five colleagues, Ben, Cara, Dev, Eli and Fay, sit in a row of five seats, numbered 1 to 5 from left to right. Ben sits in seat 3. Cara sits immediately to the right of Ben. Dev sits in seat 1. Eli does not sit next to Dev. Who sits in seat 2?

A. Ben · B. Cara · C. Dev · D. Eli · E. Fay

**Item 5.** If it rains, the match is cancelled. The match was cancelled. *It rained.*

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: True.** Priya is in the group of analysts, and everyone in that group uses spreadsheets.

**Item 2: Uncertain.** The flowers that fade quickly could all be tulips. Draw roses as a circle inside flowers, then draw "fade quickly" overlapping flowers without touching roses. It fits both statements. You can also draw it overlapping roses. Both pictures work, so you cannot tell.

**Item 3: False.** On a line from tallest to shortest: Tom, Ann, Raj. Raj is the shortest of the three, so he cannot be taller than Tom.

**Item 4: E, Fay.** Place the fixed facts: Dev in seat 1, Ben in seat 3, and Cara immediately to Ben's right in seat 4. Seats 2 and 5 are left for Eli and Fay. Seat 2 is next to Dev, and Eli does not sit next to Dev, so Eli is in seat 5 and Fay is in seat 2.

**Item 5: Uncertain.** Rain would cancel the match, but the match could have been cancelled for another reason, such as a team not turning up or a power cut at the ground. The statements do not say rain is the only cause.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | True | Uncertain | False | E | Uncertain |

- **5:** your logic is precise. Keep the drawing habit for test day, when speed tempts you to skip it.
- **4:** check the patterns table against your misses. Most misses come from reversing "all" or "if" (items 2 and 5).
- **3 or fewer:** draw every item for a week, even the easy ones. Logic errors almost always come from answering from a feeling rather than a picture.
