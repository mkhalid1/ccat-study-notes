# The CCAT explained

The Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (CCAT) is a short, fast pre-employment test from Criteria Corp. You get 50 multiple-choice questions and 15 minutes. Almost nobody finishes: Criteria says fewer than 1% of candidates answer all 50, and the average test taker gets 24 right. Your result goes to the employer as a raw score and a percentile.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Criteria Corp |
| Questions | 50, multiple choice |
| Time | 15 minutes for the whole test |
| Question types | Verbal, math and logic, spatial reasoning |
| Calculator | Not allowed |
| Penalty for wrong answers | None |
| Average result | 24 correct |
| Finishing | Fewer than 1% of candidates answer all 50 |
| What the employer sees | Raw score (number correct out of 50) and percentile |
| Pass mark | None published. Criteria offers employers custom score ranges for different positions. |
| Question pool | Drawn from an item bank of thousands of items |
| Languages | English (US) and English (UK). Other languages through the UCAT, a language-independent version. |
| Devices | Criteria says its tests can be taken on a laptop, desktop, tablet or phone, unless the instructions say a test is not compatible |

Sources: [Criteria: What to Expect on the CCAT](https://www.criteriacorp.com/candidates/ccat-prep), [Criteria: CCAT product page](https://www.criteriacorp.com/assess/cognitive-aptitude/criteria-cognitive-aptitude-test-ccat), [Criteria: How to prepare](https://www.criteriacorp.com/candidates/how-to-prepare) and the [Criteria candidate FAQ](https://www.criteriacorp.com/candidates/candidate-faqs), checked 24 November 2026.

## The three question types

Criteria names three types. These notes split "math and logic" into three pages because the methods are different.

| Criteria's type | What it covers | Guide in these notes |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal | Opposites, same meanings, analogies, sentence completion | [Verbal](sections/verbal.md) |
| Math and logic | Word problems with percentages, averages and rates | [Math word problems](sections/math-word-problems.md) |
| | Number and letter series | [Number and letter series](sections/number-series.md) |
| | Deductions from short statements, ordering, small puzzles | [Logic](sections/logic.md) |
| Spatial reasoning | Odd one out, next figure, grids, rotations | [Spatial reasoning](sections/spatial-reasoning.md) |

Criteria does not publish how many questions of each type are on a given test, or the order they come in.

## What the CCAT measures

Criteria describes the CCAT as measuring critical thinking, problem solving, learning ability and the ability to digest and apply new information. It recommends the test for mid- to high-level jobs that need problem solving, and says its predictive validity is highest for managers, software engineers and other technology workers, financial analysts and auditors.

Criteria calls the CCAT one of the most popular employment aptitude tests in the United States and says it has been given more than 10 million times.

## Why the clock matters so much

Fifteen minutes for 50 questions is 18 seconds per question. Criteria's own tip is that you have under 20 seconds per question and should make an educated guess once you have spent more than a minute on one problem.

This changes how you should prepare. On a slow test, accuracy is everything. On the CCAT, your score depends on two things together: how many questions you reach, and how many of those you get right. Someone who answers 35 questions with 28 right beats someone who answers 25 with 24 right. The [timing guide](timing.md) shows how to plan for that.

## Tests with similar names

Searches for "CCAT" mix up several different tests. Make sure you are preparing for the right one.

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **CCAT** (Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test) | The 50-question, 15-minute hiring test covered here |
| **UCAT** (Criteria's Universal Cognitive Aptitude Test) | Criteria's language-independent version: 40 items in 20 minutes, offered in 21 languages. Criteria reports a correlation of .80 with the CCAT in a sample of 252 people who took both. |
| **CCAT 7** (Canadian Cognitive Abilities Test) | A school ability test for children, from a different publisher. Unrelated to hiring. |
| **UCAT** (University Clinical Aptitude Test) | A UK and Australian medical school admissions test. Same letters, unrelated to Criteria. |

## The one rule that runs through the whole test

**Do not let any single question cost you two.** Every question is worth the same, wrong answers cost nothing extra, and the clock is the real opponent. A hard question you skip after 20 seconds is a small loss. A hard question you fight for 90 seconds is a loss plus the three or four easy questions you never reach.

## Practise the real format

These notes teach each question type on its own. The real test mixes them under one clock. Once you know the methods, take a full timed run such as the [free CCAT practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/ccat?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=ccat), which has 50 questions and a 15-minute limit like the real one. Criteria also offers [free practice tests](https://www.criteriacorp.com/candidates/practice-tests), which it describes as shortened versions that do not show the full difficulty of the real assessment.
