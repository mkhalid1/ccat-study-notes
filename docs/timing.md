# Timing and pacing

On the CCAT, timing is not one skill among many. It decides your score more than anything else. Criteria gives 15 minutes for 50 questions and says fewer than 1% of people answer them all. This page turns that into a plan.

## The budget

| | |
|---|---|
| Total time | 15 minutes (900 seconds) |
| Questions | 50 |
| Average time per question | 18 seconds |
| Criteria's own advice | Under 20 seconds per question; if you have spent over a minute on one problem, make an educated guess and move on |

Criteria's pages that we checked do not say whether you can go back to an earlier question. Plan as if every answer is final when you move on, and check your own test instructions on the day.

## A budget by question type

These are our guidance figures, not Criteria's. They add up to roughly 15 minutes for a typical mix.

| Type | Target | Give up and guess at |
|---|---|---|
| [Verbal](sections/verbal.md) | 10 to 15 seconds | 20 seconds |
| [Number and letter series](sections/number-series.md) | 15 to 25 seconds | 30 seconds |
| [Logic](sections/logic.md), true, false or uncertain | 15 to 25 seconds | 30 seconds |
| [Logic](sections/logic.md), puzzles | 30 to 40 seconds | 45 seconds |
| [Spatial](sections/spatial-reasoning.md) | 15 to 30 seconds | 30 seconds |
| [Math word problems](sections/math-word-problems.md) | 25 to 35 seconds | 40 seconds |

The fast types pay for the slow ones. If verbal items take you 30 seconds each, you will run out of time for math.

## Clock checkpoints

Use these to check your pace without doing sums during the test. They assume you aim to answer about 40 of the 50 questions, which is a realistic, strong target.

| Time left | You should be around question |
|---|---|
| 10:00 | 13 or later |
| 5:00 | 26 or later |
| 1:00 | 37 or later |

If you are well behind at a checkpoint, guess on the next long math problem you meet and pick up speed on the quick types.

## The last minute

When about one minute is left:

1. Stop trying to solve anything long.
2. Answer any quick verbal or series item you can see straight away.
3. For everything else, pick an answer rather than leave it blank. Criteria says there is no penalty for wrong answers, so a blank throws away a free chance.

If you can see that you will not reach a question in time and you are allowed to move on, choose an option and keep going. Pick the same letter every time if it saves you a second.

## Where time usually goes

- **One stubborn math problem.** The single biggest time drain. A 90-second fight with one question costs you three or four quick ones.
- **Rereading logic statements.** Draw the picture once instead of reading three times.
- **Spatial items with no rule in sight.** If the rule does not appear in 20 seconds, it rarely appears in 60.
- **Second-guessing verbal answers.** If you knew it in five seconds, click it.
- **Reading instructions during the test.** Learn each question form here, so on the day you can tell what is being asked at a glance.

## Practise against the clock

Knowing the methods is half the job. The other half is doing them at 18 seconds a question. Two habits help:

- **Time your practice sets.** Every 5-item set in these notes has a target: about 1 minute for verbal, 2 for series and logic, 2 and a half for math.
- **Take full timed runs.** The [free CCAT practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/ccat?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=ccat) uses 50 questions in 15 minutes. After each run, note which question you were on at 5, 10 and 14 minutes, and compare it with the checkpoints above.
