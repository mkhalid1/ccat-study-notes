# Glossary

Short definitions of terms used in these notes and in CCAT results.

**Analogy.** A verbal question of the form "A is to B as C is to ?". The answer pair must have the same relationship as the first pair.

**Antonym.** A word with the opposite meaning. CCAT-style verbal items often ask for the word "most nearly opposite".

**Aptitude test.** A test of your ability to learn and reason, rather than of what you already know.

**Cognitive aptitude.** General reasoning ability: learning, problem solving and handling new information. Criteria calls the CCAT a cognitive aptitude test.

**Criteria Corp.** The company that publishes the CCAT and other hiring assessments.

**Item.** One question on a test.

**Item bank.** A large pool of questions from which each candidate's test is drawn. Criteria says the CCAT uses an item bank of thousands of items.

**Modus tollens.** The logic rule "If P, then Q. Not Q. So not P." Used in [logic](sections/logic.md) items with "if" statements.

**Norm group.** The comparison group your score is ranked against to produce your percentile.

**Percentile.** The share of the norm group you scored better than. Criteria's example: the 45th percentile means you scored better than 45% of the norm group.

**Raw score.** The number of questions you got right, out of 50.

**Score range.** The band of scores an employer sets as a fit for a role. Criteria says the CCAT includes custom score ranges for many positions.

**Series.** A list of numbers, letters or figures that follows a rule. You choose what comes next.

**Spatial reasoning.** Working with shapes in your head: patterns, rotations, reflections and counting.

**Syllogism.** A pair of statements followed by a conclusion, such as "All A are B. X is A. So X is B."

**Synonym.** A word with the same or nearly the same meaning.

**UCAT (Criteria).** The Universal Cognitive Aptitude Test, Criteria's language-independent version of a cognitive aptitude test: 40 items in 20 minutes, in 21 languages.

**Uncertain.** In true, false or uncertain logic items, the answer when the statements do not decide whether the conclusion is true.
